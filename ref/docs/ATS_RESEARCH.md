## How ATS Works

### 1. Keyword Extraction
- Scans resume for exact matches from job description
- Looks for specific skills, software names, certifications
- Matches industry terminology and job titles

### 2. Scoring Algorithm
- Ranks candidates by keyword density
- Weighs exact matches higher than partial matches
- Considers both long-form and acronym versions (e.g., "Search Engine Optimization" vs "SEO")

### 3. Automatic Filtering
- Rejects resumes below threshold (typically 70-80% match)
- Filters out formatting issues (tables, graphics, headers/footers)
- Removes non-standard fonts and complex layouts

### 4. Human Review
- Only top-scored CVs (20-30%) reach recruiters
- Recruiters view pre-scored and ranked candidates
- Most resumes viewed at least once (90%+), but briefly

## Critical Insights

### From Forbes (2024):
> "Use the job description as a guide for your resume. It contains all the keywords and phrases that the company's ATS is likely programmed to pick up. Your task is to mirror the language of the job listing in your resume."

### From Industry Research:
- **Exact phrase matching** is critical - "Project Management" ≠ "Managing Projects"
- **Keyword stuffing** triggers red flags - natural integration required
- **Standard formatting** essential - avoid tables, graphics, headers/footers
- **Chronological or Hybrid formats** work best
- **Both long-form and acronyms** should be included (e.g., "AI" and "Artificial Intelligence")

## Ethical Considerations

### What We Should Do:
- ✅ Help users include **genuine skills** they have
- ✅ Optimize **formatting and keyword placement**
- ✅ Mirror **job description language** authentically
- ✅ Educate users about **ATS realities**

### What We Should NOT Do:
- ❌ Encourage **keyword stuffing** (triggers red flags)
- ❌ Add **false skills** to CVs
- ❌ Guarantee **ATS bypass** (overpromising)
- ❌ Game the system **unethically**

**Our Approach:** Help users present their genuine skills in ATS-friendly ways, not fabricate qualifications.
